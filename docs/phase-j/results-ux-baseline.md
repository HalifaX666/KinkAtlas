# Phase J.4 Results UX baseline

## Scope

This baseline records the Results experience at `c6a15d9a4c7eb7d78da7cfa6f87b1aac66cb6ffc`. It describes presentation only. Assessment scoring, recommendation construction, role-set optimization, and historical snapshot semantics were not changed during the baseline review.

## Previous reading order

1. “A map, not a verdict” and the ephemeral-session notice.
2. Ten normalized “Strongest dimensions” meters.
3. Current editable role cards, labelled “Your Role Cards.”
4. A collapsed “Build your role set” disclosure containing both Suggested Role Set and Your Role Set management.
5. Save and Share actions.
6. Role Discovery, Reflection, blind spots, boundaries, communication, and learning sections.

## Confirmed comprehension issues

- The first substantive takeaway was a technical dimension display rather than a plain-language account of what the answers supported.
- Suggested Role Set was available only inside a collapsed editor. A first-time reader could see the editable set before seeing the assessment-generated starting set or its rationale.
- “Your Role Cards” did not clearly distinguish the user’s editable collection from the immutable assessment suggestion.
- Role Discovery cards showed confidence labels, but the role-level explanation of what confidence meant was not visible inside their evidence disclosure.
- Full Reflection was available only after opening Share and then selecting its mode. It was not present in Results navigation.
- Section navigation required long-page scrolling, with no compact way to return to the principal interpretation areas.

## Existing strengths retained

- The hero already states that role alignments are suggestions, not assigned identities.
- Alignment, confidence, and evidence breadth are separate and correctly defined.
- Role-card evidence is derived from the existing `RoleResult`; no prose is inferred from a role name.
- Suggested Role Set and Role Discovery remain separate products of the same assessment evidence.
- Your Role Set already supports reorder, primary, replace, remove, add, and empty states.
- Manual roles already avoid assessment score and confidence claims.
- Reflection, readiness, boundaries, and consent are explicitly independent from role alignment.
- Historical views are read-only and are not silently rescored.

## Accessibility and responsive baseline

The page used semantic headings, native details/summary controls, labelled meters, and keyboard-operable buttons and links. The primary usability concern was information order and discoverability rather than missing input semantics. The existing long page also lacked an in-page results navigation landmark.

## Full Reflection boundary

Full Reflection is generated by the existing deterministic share-results engine and includes user-controlled sections. Its wording can sound mechanical in places, but changing narrative selection or fixtures is J.5 work and is not part of J.4.
