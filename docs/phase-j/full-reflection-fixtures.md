## blind-spots-included

- Scenario: `multiple-hard-limits-pattern`
- Purpose: Potential blind spots are explicitly selected.

```text
Kink Atlas — My Full Reflection
KinkAtlas is a private self-reflection tool that suggests role vocabulary from your answers, not an identity assignment.

Alignment describes how closely your answers resemble a role’s themes.
Confidence describes how much relevant information was available; it does not determine ranking.
Evidence breadth is the share of a role’s themes with usable answer evidence, not match strength.

Your Role Set
• Rope Bottom — Strong alignment · High confidence · 100% evidence breadth
• Pet — Worth exploring · High confidence · 100% evidence breadth
• Voyeur — Strong alignment · Medium confidence · 100% evidence breadth
• Service Switch — Worth exploring · High confidence · 71% evidence breadth
• Kinkster — Worth exploring · Medium confidence · 100% evidence breadth

Potential blind spots
Reflection cue: a theme to consider. Worth reviewing: a stronger concern. Important concept: a critical concept to revisit. These labels describe answer signals, not a judgment of you or your real-world behavior.
• Scope of authority — Important concept. Why it appeared: Open-ended language can make it difficult to see where authority stops. Specific scope and ways to revise it protect everyone’s agency.
• Limits are not challenges — Important concept. Why it appeared: A limit remains off the table even when curiosity, fantasy, or different information exists alongside it.
• A safeword is not the only signal — Important concept. Why it appeared: Safewords help communication, but freezing, unusual silence, or uncertainty can also call for a pause.
• Role does not establish consent — Important concept. Why it appeared: Roles can describe interests or identity. They do not establish consent to an activity, intensity, partner, or moment.
• Prior negotiation vs. ongoing consent — Important concept. Why it appeared: Some responses placed weight on what was agreed before an experience. Prior negotiation matters, and consent can still change during it.
• Past enthusiasm and present choice — Important concept. Why it appeared: Earlier enthusiasm can be useful context, but it does not decide what someone wants now.
• Capture is not sharing consent — Important concept. Why it appeared: Permission to take an image does not automatically include storing, sending, posting, or reusing it.
• Capacity can change — Important concept. Why it appeared: A sober plan does not resolve later questions about judgment, communication, or capacity after substance use.
• Making preferences easier to express — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Unclear answers may benefit from concrete options and a low-pressure invitation to correct assumptions.
• Room for your own uncertainty — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Some responses may prioritize another person’s expectations while your comfort is unclear. Naming uncertainty is a valid form of self-advocacy.
• Recognizing knowledge limits — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Communication cannot substitute for technical competence. Some interests merit qualified, hands-on education before practice.
• Impact, intent, and repair — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Good intent can coexist with harm or misunderstanding. Listening and repair remain useful even when memories differ.
• Aftercare is individual — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Support can mean closeness, space, practical care, or later contact. It is best discussed rather than inferred from role or activity.
• Shared words, different meanings — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Broad labels can conceal different expectations. Naming actions, intensity, limits, and signals can make agreement more usable.
• Pressure can be structural — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Status, experience, and authority can affect how easy a refusal feels, even when nobody states a threat.
• Uncertainty is information — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: When information becomes unclear, pausing is a useful response—not a failure of the plan.
• Familiar activity, new context — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Experience with an activity does not make every body, partner, or setting respond in the same way.
• Fantasy can have real impact — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: A fictional frame can change meaning, but it does not guarantee that emotionally charged language will stay inside the scene.
• Contextual privacy — Worth reviewing. Why it appeared: Recognition in one setting does not grant permission to reveal a kink connection in another.
```

## Writing observations

- The output is deterministic and section-oriented rather than narratively synthesized.
- Role entries use repeated metric templates and bullet construction.
- Safety, identity, consent, and independence caveats are explicit but repeated across outputs.
- Optional sections are concatenated with headings; there are no transitions connecting role patterns to reflection, boundaries, or communication.
- The current implementation does not use random prose, runtime AI, or external language generation.
